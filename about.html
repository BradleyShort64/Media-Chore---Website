Our Story

Where the name comes from.

Media Chore is a play on house chores — the jobs that have to get done, that nobody looks forward to, and that quietly pile up until somebody finally deals with them.

For a lot of business owners, marketing is exactly that. It is important, everybody knows it matters, and it still ends up at the bottom of the list behind the work that pays this month’s bills. The posts do not go out. The website still has last year’s prices on it. The shoot gets moved again. Not because anybody stopped caring, but because running a business already fills the day.

That is the gap we exist to fill. Media Chore is for businesses that see marketing as a chore and need somebody to take it off their hands — properly, consistently, and without having to be chased. You should not have to become a marketer to have marketing that works.

We started operating as Media Chore in 2025 and registered the business officially in 2026. We work out of Hillcrest in Durban and take on work across South Africa, from founders getting a first brand off the ground to established companies who simply want the marketing handled.

Our Approach

How we think about marketing.

A lot of what the marketing industry sells is confidence dressed up as certainty. We would rather be useful than impressive.

The industry has a habit of leading with the things that are easy to show off — the software, the acronyms, the dashboards, the promise that some new tool has changed everything. Most of that is not the job. The job is understanding a business well enough to say something true about it, then saying it consistently, in the places its customers actually are.

So we start with the business, not the deliverable. How do you make money? Who are you actually talking to? What is genuinely holding the marketing back — is it the message, the consistency, or simply that nobody has had time to press publish? The answer changes what we recommend, and sometimes it means recommending less than you came in asking for.

We also think specialisation beats breadth. Somebody who rides horses will write better equine content than a generalist reading a brief about it. Somebody who has worked in architecture will shoot a property differently. That is why our work is organised around industry specialists rather than a single house style applied to everybody.

Technology and AI sit inside that workflow where they earn their place — research, structure, iteration, getting to a better second draft faster. They are tools. The direction, the judgement and the relationship stay with people, and we are not going to pretend otherwise to sound advanced.
